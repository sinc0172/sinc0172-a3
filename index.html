History of The Beatles

The Beatles were an English rock band, formed in Liverpool in 1960. They became the most commercially successful and critically acclaimed act in the history of popular music. Made up of four members -- John Lennon on rhythm guitar, Paul McCartney on bass, George Harrison on guitar, Ringo Starr on drums, and all four on vocals -- the band created some of the most iconic songs in music history.

The band was officially formed in 1960, with the namesake of the band being derived from popular American performer, Buddy Holly and The Crickets. The Beatles were discovered by their manager, Brian Epstein, at the Cavern Club in Liverpool in 1961. Soon after their meeting, the band gained immense popularity in the UK and eventually became a global phenomenon with their single "I Want to Hold Your Hand" (1963). They toured continuously throughout the early-to-mid 1960s, having record-breaking success at venues like Shea Stadium and The Ed Sullivan Show, until eventually ending their touring saga in 1966.

After they finished touring, the band continued to create music, discovering a passion for experimental sounds and innovative recording techniques while in the studio. The records they released during this period was unlike anything they had created before, utlizing instruments and effects unheard of in previous eras. Their final album 'Let it Be' was released in 1970, effectively marking the end of the momentuous 7 year journey together. The members of the band continued to release music on their own, sometimes collaborating on tracks for their solo projects.

In 1995, the remaining members of the band reunited to record new music for The Beatles' Anthology project, utitilizing demo tracks from the late John Lennon. Later in 2023, the band released "Now and Then", the official last song by the band.
